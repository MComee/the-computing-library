# Educational Longevity & Relevance Classifications

This is a cross-cutting index for all **261** entries in the Computing Library. It complements the 19 subject categories without removing, duplicating, or renumbering books. Each entry can have multiple labels. These are **curatorial, provisional labels**, based on titles, bibliographic summaries, and subject context rather than a verified edition-by-edition review. They describe *why the text remains useful*, not its reading difficulty or an endorsement of every claim in the text.

## Classification definitions

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundational / Timeless** | Mathematical, computational, logical, or engineering ideas transferable across programming languages and platforms. The *principles* endure, although presentations can age. |
| **Conceptually Enduring** | An implementation, methodology, or model used to teach generalizable design and reasoning skills. |
| **Historical / Canonical** | Important for understanding computing history, influential original works, architecture, hacker culture, or past security standards; may not describe current practice. |
| **Contemporary / Applied** | Useful for current or recent engineering practice, subject to checking the edition and current standards. |
| **Version-Sensitive** | Concrete APIs, tools, language versions, exploits, configuration procedures, or platform advice may have aged; always confirm against current primary sources. |

A label is **not** a promise of current correctness. In particular, *historically important* is not the same as *safe to implement today*, and *foundational* does not mean that every factual claim has survived later research. Psychological and consciousness literature should be considered with attention to empirical support, not automatically treated as established science.
